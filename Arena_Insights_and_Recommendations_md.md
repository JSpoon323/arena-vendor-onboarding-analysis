# Crypto.com Arena: Vendor Onboarding Insights & Recommendations

**Analyst:** Spoon · **Tools:** Excel (data cleaning, live formula engine, dashboard), Claude (AI assistant)
**Scope:** 370 vendors · 7 onboarding coordinators · onboarding status snapshot
**Context:** A designed sourcing and procurement scenario. Onboarding the full supplier base that services an arena, from a raw 400-vendor list cleaned to 370.

---

## Executive summary

Onboarding is progressing but not yet safe. **38% of the 370 vendors are fully onboarded (140)** and **74% are on track (273, complete or in progress)**, which is healthy forward momentum. But **97 vendors (26%) are in a red-flag state**, delayed or unresponsive, and they carry **$8,711,400 in annual spend.**

The number that should set the agenda is narrower and sharper. Of the **129 critical suppliers**, the services the arena cannot open without, **32 are delayed or unresponsive, and they represent $5,550,000 in essential-services spend that is not yet secured.** That is the real headline. The question is not "are we behind?" but "are the doors-open services locked down?" Right now, they are not.

The good news: the risk is concentrated and nameable. Two coordinators are carrying the onboarding effort, three specific coordinators hold every poor-communication flag, and the ten highest-dollar at-risk suppliers can be listed on one screen. That makes this a targeting problem, not a scramble.

Marisol and Trevor lead on completion (57% each), but here is the nuance a good analyst catches: they also carry the highest at-risk exposure, because they were assigned the heaviest concentration of critical, high-dollar suppliers. ## Red flags: the concentration patterns

Two patterns jump out of the data that a status average would hide.

**Poor communication is concentrated in three coordinators.** All 23 poor-communication flags sit with Priya Kapoor (8), Hannah Whitlock (8), and Andre Boseman (7). The other four coordinators have none. When a problem clusters this cleanly, it is rarely coincidence. It points to either a workload imbalance, a batch of genuinely unresponsive vendors assigned to those three, or a coaching opportunity. It is worth diagnosing why before treating the symptom.

**Every Delayed (EOD) flag belongs to one coordinator.** All 12 end-of-day-deadline delays are Damon Fields's. A single coordinator holding 100% of the most time-sensitive delays signals a bottleneck. Damon may be carrying a batch of hard-deadline vendors that needs either reinforcement or reassignment before those deadlines pass.

## Recommendations

Ordered by risk to opening day.

### 1. Triage the $5.55M in critical at-risk suppliers first
The 32 delayed-or-unresponsive critical suppliers are the only items that can stop the doors from opening. Escalate the top-ten at-risk list immediately, starting with Monarch Mechanical ($434K) and Precision Electrical ($408K), with direct outreach and firm deadlines. Everything else is secondary to securing HVAC, electrical, security, fire and life safety, and elevators.

### 2. Diagnose the poor-communication cluster
Priya, Hannah, and Andre hold all 23 poor-comms flags. Meet with these three to determine cause (workload, vendor quality, or process) and provide escalation support or reassign the truly unresponsive vendors. Solving this clears the softest, most fixable part of the risk.

### 3. Break the EOD bottleneck on Damon's book
All 12 end-of-day delays are Damon's. Reinforce or partially reassign his time-critical vendors so no deadline lapses simply because one person is the single point of contact.

### 4. Convert Yuki's Received backlog for a cheap completion bump
Yuki has 40 vendors received but only 10 completed: low criticality, high volume, mid-process. A focused documentation push here raises the overall completion rate quickly and safely, with minimal risk exposure.

### 5. Codify what Marisol and Trevor do
The two highest performers are also carrying the hardest books. Capture their onboarding approach as the standard operating procedure and use it to lift the middle of the pack. The best process improvement is already inside the team.

---

## What success looks like

Opening readiness is not "100% of vendors onboarded." It is "100% of critical suppliers secured." The target is closing the 32 critical at-risk suppliers ($5.55M) before the arena opens, in priority order by spend and service type. The overall 38% completion figure will climb on its own as the pipeline drains. The critical-supplier number is the one that determines whether the building can operate.

## Method note

This dashboard is fully live. A cleaned 370-vendor list feeds seven coordinator worksheets. A central formula engine (`INDIRECT` plus `COUNTIFS` and `SUMIFS` across all seven tabs) rolls each coordinator's status counts, critical-at-risk count, and at-risk spend into one table. The KPI cards, the completion-by-coordinator bar, the status donut, the critical-focus split, and the top-at-risk leaderboard (`VSTACK` plus `FILTER` plus `SORT` plus `TAKE`) all read from that engine, so the moment a coordinator updates a vendor's status, every number and chart re-ranks itself. Nothing on the dashboard is hardcoded. The raw data began as a deliberately dirty 400-row list (exact duplicates and fuzzy name variants) and was cleaned to 370 via Remove Duplicates and Fuzzy Lookup, the data-quality step that makes every downstream number trustworthy.
